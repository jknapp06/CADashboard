Table 1 shows the counts of LEAs eligible for Differentiated Assistance
(DA) for three years. Charter schools became eligible for DA in 2023.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{da_summary_files/figure-pdf/indicators-1.pdf}}

The figure above shows the breakdown for which indicators districts were
eligible for DA. Reminder: LEAs are eligible for DA due to low
performance in two or more priorities for one or more student groups.
The chart above can be read as follows: in 2019, 2 districts were
eligible for DA in part due to low performance in -- high rates of --
chronic absenteeism. In 2022, that count was 6 districts, and in 2023, 3
districts were eligible in part due to the chronic absenteeism
indicator. Low performance can mean different things for each indicator:
low performance in chronic absenteeism and suspension means that those
rates are high, but low performance in academic, graduation, and
college/career indicators mean low aggregated test scores or low rates.

The college/career indicator was not reported in 2022 so no districts
were eligible for DA based on that indicator.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{da_summary_files/figure-pdf/student_group_indicators-1.pdf}}

The length of the bars in the chart above refers to the number of
districts eligible for DA in Solano County. The chart shows the
breakdown by student groups. It can be read as: in 2019 (the darkest,
bottom bar for each group) homeless youth factored into one district's
eligibility. In 2022, 3 districts were eligible for DA, at least in
part, due to low performance of indicators for the homeless youth
student group. In 2023, outcomes from the homeless youth student group
factored into the eligibility of 2 districts. Another example is that
for all three years reported, the socioeconomically disadvantaged
student group factored into the DA eligibility for one district --
though not necessarily the same district or indicators each year.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{da_summary_files/figure-pdf/academic_multiples-1.pdf}}

The graphs above show the numbers of districts eligible for DA based in
part on academic indicators for the last five years. Academic indicators
include student performance on English Language Arts (ELA), Math, and
the English Learner Progress Index (ELPI) -- for English Learners only.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{da_summary_files/figure-pdf/absenteeism_multiples-1.pdf}}

The graphs above show the numbers of districts eligible for DA based in
part on chronic absenteeism for the last five years. A student is
counted as chronically absent when they miss 10\% or more of the days
they are expected to attend school. Most student groups in most LEAs
improved in their rates of chronic absenteeism helping them to be
assigned colors at high performance levels, but absolute rates of
chronic absenteeism are still high for most LEAs.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{da_summary_files/figure-pdf/absenteeism_status_multiples-1.pdf}}

The above figure shows three years of chronic absenteeism rates. Each
graph shows the distribution of student groups by chronic absenteeism
rate.The vertical blue line shows the median rate for the ``all
students'' group in districts in Solano County. In 2023, the median
chronic absenteeism rate was 11\%, and we can see the distribution for
student groups matches up with this with most student groups to the left
of this line. In 2022, the median rate was 33.9\%, but the distribution
shows a large number of student groups with higher rates. In 2023,
chronic absenteeism rates went down across the board with the median
rate for ``all students'' declining, the distribution of student groups
shifting to the right, and the highest rates decreasing, but it is still
much higher than pre-pandemic levels. Additionally, a large number of
student groups are to the right of the median line suggesting that there
are many student groups with potentially small numbers of students that
have a higher rate of chronic absenteeism than the median.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{da_summary_files/figure-pdf/suspension_multiples-1.pdf}}

The graphs above show the number of districts eligible for DA based in
part on suspension rates of that student group for the last five years.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{da_summary_files/figure-pdf/college/career-1.pdf}}

The figure above shows the counts for districts eligible for DA at least
in part based on the college/career indicator. It is broken down by
student groups indicating which groups played a factor in DA eligibility
across the county.
